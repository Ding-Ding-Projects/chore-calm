# Chore coaching concept

## What the concept is for

Chore Calm is a concept for a calm companion that teaches routines without yelling, threats,
shame, or relationship ultimatums. It can present one next step, explain why the step matters,
remind someone at a chosen time, and offer a recovery path when a step is missed.

The marketing promise includes no mess quota and no annoyance ledger. Ordinary messes can happen
again and again, while the companion stays patient and offers one safe repair at a time. A forgotten
chore is retaught, not turned into a lecture, and the concept never uses relationship ultimatums.

ADHD can make chores harder through initiation, working memory, sequencing, sensory load, and
recovery from mistakes. Those difficulties can make a familiar chore feel unexpectedly large or
unclear. A useful companion should reduce the amount a person has to hold in mind, keep the next
action visible, and allow a pause or restart without treating a missed step as a moral failure.

## Calm interaction principles

1. Name the next action, not a character judgment.
2. Keep instructions short, concrete, and ordered.
3. Make pause, skip, retry, and ask-for-help actions visible.
4. State what was not completed without shame or threat.
5. Offer recovery from mistakes instead of escalating the conversation.
6. Never imply that another person's anger is under the visitor's control.

## What it cannot promise

The companion cannot control another person's anger, prevent another person from shouting, or
guarantee a peaceful home. If someone is threatened, unsafe, or at risk, the concept should point
to trusted people and appropriate local support rather than pretending a chore reminder can solve
the situation. It is not medical treatment, does not diagnose ADHD, and does not replace a
clinician or other professional support.

## Static-page boundary

The current concept page explains the concept, shows accessible step-by-step examples, and lets a
visitor inspect calm wording in English, Cantonese, or a bilingual presentation. A static page can
store a visitor's display preferences in browser storage, but it cannot honestly provide a secure
application credential vault, background reminders after the page is closed, an operating-system
notification service, or control over another person's device.

The page implementation must label demonstrations as demonstrations. It must not simulate a real
reminder being delivered, claim that a notification was sent, or present a future product control
as shipped behavior.

## Accessibility expectations

The page should provide keyboard operation, visible focus, readable contrast, reduced-motion
behavior, captions or text alternatives for every animation, touch targets suitable for narrow
screens, and a non-animated path through every explanation. A visitor must be able to understand
the core message without sound, motion, colour, hover, or a pointer.
